# Ballistic motion reconstruction

The live ballistic branch in the original projectile update at `0x0049b720` distinguishes ballistic weapons through bit 1 of weapon offset `0x111`. With zero flight time, it reaches `0x0049bce3`; with a nonzero, unexpired timer it reaches `0x0049bc37`. Both move the shell by its current velocity, add a game-level vector at `GAME+0x37ecc/0x37ed0/0x37ed4`, then subtract gravity at `GAME+0x14263` from vertical velocity. Position and velocity use signed 32-bit fixed-point values and wrap on overflow. The drift vector's derivation remains untraced; it must not yet be assumed to equal a particular wind-speed field.

`ballistic_motion.gd` implements this isolated update. `native_ballistic_reference.py` executes original instructions from `0x0049bb60`, supplying ballistic flags and registers, and replaces the boundary at `0x0049bd86` with return before collision dispatch. No exercised arithmetic is replaced. This is not a full projectile or world simulation oracle.

`compare_native_ballistics.gd` matches 1,120 cases: 1,000 deterministic inputs spanning signed positions/velocities, gravity and drift, both zero and nonzero unexpired flight timers, plus 120 consecutive steps of a shell arc. Five normal regression checks cover update order, input preservation, the arc endpoint, drift and integer wrap. The compact result is `native-ballistic-validation.json`; raw traces stay under ignored `local/ballistics/`.

## Further evidence and unresolved behavior

The map loader uses gravity field `+0xd3c` in its descriptor, and TNT header field 13 for legacy terrain. `map_environment.py` now reconstructs selection and conversion: for TNT version 0x2000 or later, a nonnegative descriptor gravity overrides the legacy field; otherwise zero legacy gravity selects raw `0x1fdb` (8155). Explicit descriptor zero stays zero. Conversion multiplies the signed integer by 65536 then the executable's binary64 1/900 constant with x87 precision before truncation and signed32 storage. All 96 supplied loader cases match original instructions at `0x4837e9..0x483842`.

The installed Comet Catcher OTA specifies gravity60, minwindspeed10 and maxwindspeed15. Its prepared scene now stores raw gravity4369 and those wind bounds, with versioned environment metadata. The launcher regenerates older viewer bundles. The earlier isolated ballistic fixtures deliberately supply default gravity8155; they do not represent Comet Catcher trajectories. Map environment selection has six normal tests. Raw descriptor data remains local; only parsed environment numbers are added to the ignored bundle.

Ballistic aiming is at `0x0049a890`, called by the weapon range check at `0x0049aa80`. `ballistic_aim.gd` now reconstructs its two candidate angles from source-minus-target fixed-point coordinates, velocity, supplied gravity and minimum angle. The routine selects the first acceptable candidate, requiring it to exceed the minimum and not exceed the executable's approximately pi/4 constant. Failure is the low-word sentinel `0x8000`; callers must not interpret it as a firing angle. Its conversion uses the executable's binary64 constants 32768 and 0.318309886183791 before truncation.

`native_ballistic_aim.py` executes the complete unmodified aim routine and original CRT math. The Godot reconstruction matches all 1,236 cases: 1,200 deterministic varied inputs and 36 boundary inputs covering zero/tiny horizontal distances, higher/lower targets, minimum-angle rejection and adjacent fixed-point distances across maximum reach. There are 494 accepted cases. Nine fixed native examples run in normal verification. The compact report is `native-ballistic-aim-validation.json`. These comparisons cover positive speed and gravity up to 16000; Godot uses binary64 intermediates instead of a general x87 emulator, so untested precision boundaries and abnormal inputs remain unproven.

Ballistic launch at `0x0049cde0` now has a native-compared velocity primitive, `ballistic_launch.gd`. Its horizontal scalar is the original cosine helper applied to pitch and speed; X/Z are negative sine/cosine components of heading at that scalar. Vertical velocity is the original sine helper applied to pitch/speed minus gravity times the unsigned controller accumulator divided by weapon speed (integer quotient). The accumulator is at controller `+0x10`, pitch at `+0x18`, heading at `+0x16`; the weapon pointer is at `+0x0c` and scalar speed at weapon `+0x68`.

`native_ballistic_launch.py` executes `0x0049ce4f` through `0x0049cecc` with supplied controller state, returning after velocity stores and stack cleanup before lifetime setup. All 1,000 velocity cases match, spanning quantized headings/pitches, four weapon speeds, three gravity values and unsigned accumulator division boundaries. See `native-ballistic-launch-validation.json`.

The controller offset's initialization is now recovered at `0x0049e070`: it queries the muzzle world position through `0x0043e240` and the aiming-origin position through `0x0043e2e0`, then stores `trunc(signed32(muzzle_z - aim_z) * 1.25)` with signed32 wrap at controller+0x10. It uses Z separation, not Euclidean distance. `initial_offset` matches 300 original arithmetic-block cases, bringing the launch comparison to 1,300 cases and normal launch checks to 14. The aiming-origin helper calls AimFromPrimary (or the corresponding other weapon callback), falling back to QueryPrimary if the callback leaves its output at -1. Native piece-to-world transforms and any subsequent writes to the offset still need tracing before complete controller integration. The variable previously called a travel accumulator must not be interpreted as simply elapsed time.

The minimum-angle input comes from weapon `minbarrelangle`, loaded with default -11.25 degrees into weapon+0xc8. Its conversion is now native-compared and prepared as runtime.minimum_barrel_angle for all193 weapons; see WEAPON_SCALARS.md. Multiplication uses binary64 constant0.017453292519943278 with float32 storage (the default is -0.19634954631328583 radians). This is ready for world aiming integration.

The drift vector's writer is wind update `0x00490c40`. Its timing, RNG transitions, strength/heading and X/Z drift now match 600 native cases; see WIND.md. It updates X/Z using negative twice the original sine/cosine helper results from wind heading `GAME+0x37ed8` and strength `GAME+0x37eda`. World scheduling, initial seeds and the Y component's initialization still need integration.

The primitive is not connected to Raider world firing yet. Integration still needs verified launch angles/velocity, map gravity and drift, lifetime/expiration rules, terrain and unit collision, and splash damage. The current EMG host's path-length cap is not evidence for ballistic shell lifetime and must not be reused without tracing. Raider retaliation and opponent AI remain outstanding.

Run `python tools/native_ballistic_reference.py`, then Godot `--headless --path godot --script res://compare_native_ballistics.gd`. The normal test is `test_ballistic_motion.gd`; both are included in the appropriate `tools/verify.ps1` modes.
# Weapon timer expiration

Launch deadline reconstruction: original `0x49cecc` through `0x49cf40` adds the unsigned16 weapon timer to the current tick for ordinary shells. With burnblow, it instead truncates horizontal muzzle-to-target distance, divides by the signed horizontal launch speed and adds that duration to the current tick. Y separation is ignored. The final deadline wraps to32 bits. `BallisticLaunch.deadline` matches600 native cases with positive horizontal speed and ordinary coordinate differences; zero divisors and extreme distance overflow remain outside the supported domain. Four regression checks bring the launch suite to18. See `native-ballistic-deadline-validation.json`. This helper is not yet connected to playable Raider firing.

The ballistic update at `0x49bc0c` reads weapon+0xe6, which is **weapontimer**, not the distinct flighttime field. Earlier integration fixtures named this input `flight_ticks`; that label was inaccurate. A zero value bypasses expiry. Otherwise the unsigned current tick at or beyond the unsigned deadline triggers expiration before motion: burnblow (weapon flag0x800000) invokes impact dispatch; without it the shell receives an expiration visual notification and is marked removed. A live shell retains the previously verified integration behavior.

`BallisticMotion.expiration` matches600 original branch executions, checking the event and all position/velocity components through live and expired paths. Both burnblow values, zero/nonzero timers, equal/adjacent deadlines and unsigned extremes are covered. Impact and visual callbacks are captured by stubs; their downstream behavior is excluded. Five new regression checks bring the motion suite to10. See `native-ballistic-lifetime-validation.json`. Deadline creation, timer data conversion and combat-host integration remain separate work; this does not make Raider firing playable yet.
